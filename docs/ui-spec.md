# UI spec

This is the visual contract for the two screens. The prototypes in `prototypes/` show the starting point; where they differ from this file, this file wins. Every colour, size and font comes from `design/tokens.css`.

Both screens are fixed 1920×1080 stages scaled to fit the monitor. They never scroll. Background is `--bg-canvas` (#050606). Font is Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (Google Fonts), chosen for low vision. Every colour comes from `design/tokens.css`.

Do not add encodings, legends or text that are not listed here. The design avoids text, rounded cards scattered over the screen, and any symbol the user would have to learn; when in doubt, follow what Jira and GitHub already do.

## Shared rules

- **Status colours follow Jira and GitHub, filled:** To do `#5E6968`, In progress `#3a8ce0`, Blocked `#e54c4a`, Done `#16a356`. Cancelled is a hollow ring `1.5px #8E9897`.
- **Ticket type is a white glyph inside the circle:** bookmark for Story, check for Task, bug for Bug. Glyph size is about 42% of the circle.
- **Skobeloff** (`#007474` family) is reserved for Elfoqsy and her agents. It is not a status colour and is not used for structure.
- **Structure lines** (rings, orbits) are `1.5px #444E4D`. Brighter was rejected as too loud, darker as invisible.
- **Labels** next to tickets are the ticket key only. The real summary appears on hover (tooltip, summary first, then type, status, points).
- **Headline** at top left: date and time in `text-muted` 18px, then one sentence at 60px weight 800 that answers "does anything need me". Its colour is the colour of the most urgent thing on that screen.

## Floating layers

Everything that floats over the screens follows these rules, and nothing floats otherwise.

| Layer | Kind | Opens | Placed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The grown dial (B+) | dial | hover, or keyboard focus inside it | in place, 1.7× from its top left |
| A dot's title in the grown dial; "Find a ticket Ctrl K" | tooltip, inside the dial | hover or keyboard focus on the dot or button | right of the dot (the button: left) |
| A ticket, PR, +N or done dot on the sprint map | tooltip | hover or keyboard focus | right of it |
| The keyboard hint on the right screen | tooltip | hover or keyboard focus | under its icon |
| Any control's tip (`data-tip`, in place of the browser's `title`) | tooltip | hover, or keyboard focus outside text fields | under the control |
| The status menu | menu | a click on the status chip | under the chip |
| The editor's text style menu | menu | a click on "Normal text ▾" | under the button |
| Her settings ("What Elfoqsy decides for you") | menu (a panel) | the settings button | under the button |
| A pane's ⋯ menu | menu | the ⋯ button | under it, right-aligned |
| Find a ticket | search (a dialog) | Ctrl K, or the magnifier under the dial's expand button | centred on the screen near its top, over a dimmed backdrop |

- **One at a time.** Opening a layer closes every other open layer, except the layers it sits in: a dot's title is a tooltip inside the grown dial, so the dial stays grown under it. Opening a menu or the search shrinks the dial and hides every tooltip. A tooltip never closes a menu, panel or dialog: while one is open, only the tooltips inside it show, so the pointer crossing the screen cannot shut what he opened.
- **Closing.** A menu, panel or dialog closes on a press anywhere outside it (the backdrop counts as outside), on Escape, or when another layer opens; a tooltip and the grown dial also close when the pointer or keyboard focus leaves what opened them. Escape closes only the top layer, and nothing else hears it: it does not close the ticket or cancel an editor behind the layer. Escape, and choosing an item of a menu, give focus back to where it was when the layer opened; a press outside leaves focus where he pressed.
- **Stacking.** From the tokens, bottom to top: `--z-dial` (the grown dial), `--z-tooltip`, `--z-menu`, `--z-search`. Every layer but the grown dial is drawn in one root on the stage, over both screens' clipping, so the order holds everywhere.
- **Placing.** A layer goes beside its anchor on its own side, aligned to the anchor's start (a ⋯ menu to its end). It flips to the opposite side when it does not fit, then tries the other two, and shifts along its side to stay 8px inside the 1920 × 1080 screen and off any other open layer. It is hidden until placed, and placed again when it changes size, its anchor moves (a scroll) or the window resizes. A tooltip is at most 320px wide (`--tooltip-max-width`) and wraps; it never takes the pointer.
- **The grown dial's labels** avoid each other and every dot: each tries twelve directions around its dot at increasing distances, the open ticket's first, then larger dots; one that has to go farther than 12px is tied to its dot by a hairline. A label with no clear place is not drawn (its tooltip still names the dot); none was found in 500 test sprints of 4 to 15 tickets.
- **No browser tooltips.** Nothing uses the browser's own `title` tooltip, which nothing can place or stack: a control's tip is its `data-tip`, shown as a tooltip layer (one per screen), and hidden by a press as the browser's was. The map and the dial have richer tooltips of their own (key, title, workflow status, points).
- **Tested** by a browser check run after every step of an interaction script and of the real-use script: it fails if two visible floating layers overlap (a tooltip over its own layer aside), if one is off the screen, if more than one is open, if one is stacked other than its token says, or if any element has a browser tooltip (`title`). A unit test fails if a `title` attribute comes back into the source.

## Layout and panes


- **Five panes**, each a component that fills the box it is given and picks its form from that box (a ResizeObserver on its own slot; CSS container queries for what is only style), never from the screen: Sprint, Elfoqsy, Conversation, Agent map, Terminals. Text never scales; a slot's size changes geometry and which form shows.
- **Sizes** (`apps/web/src/panes/spec.ts`): preferred and minimum width Sprint 840 and 240, Elfoqsy 342 and 330, Conversation 618 and 380, Agent map 640 and 300, Terminals 1180 and 300; every pane at least 240 tall so that stacks fit. Spare width goes to Conversation and Terminals first, then Sprint and Agent map; Elfoqsy grows only when alone.
- **Forms.** Sprint: full from 760 wide and 700 tall; short under 560 tall and compact from 480 wide, which draw the map fitted to the slot (the same geometry smaller, each key as its digits, a label beside its dot only where it clears the others; compact also holds a ticket, brief, handoff or the create form, short does not); list from 240 (open tickets by priority, a title under its key below 300 wide); rail under 240. Terminals: four from 1000 wide and 700 tall; two (stacked) from 500, one if under 560 tall; one with agent tabs from 380; tabs only under 380 (the agents as rows, one opens its pane in place with a back button). Agent map: full; compact under 500 wide or 600 tall (the geometry scales to the room, never under 0.4, the portrait with it; the text does not). Elfoqsy and Conversation: one form each, and the geometry gives way to the height before anything is cut (Elfoqsy's portrait shrinks, then goes, then her usage; the composer takes one line).
- **A ticket, a brief, a handoff or the create form** show in Sprint's full and compact forms; in a slot too small for them ("short", list) the slot's own form shows (the fitted map or the list), with one line saying the ticket needs a slot 480 wide and 560 tall, and a link back to the map.
- **With no layout saved** both screens are today's: the same columns, rails and collapse buttons, laid out as they always were, pixel for pixel (the browser tests compare both screens in ten states, `apps/web/e2e/baseline.mjs`). Elfoqsy and the conversation share one panel there; in a layout of their own they each get a panel.
- **Free layout.** The layout is saved in the daemon, as an event (`layout.changed`, folded into `state.layout`): both windows hold the same one at once and after a reload; `null` is no layout saved, today's screens. A screen is a row of columns, each a stack of panes; each pane is on exactly one screen. The geometry is `packages/state/src/layout.ts` (the daemon refuses a layout that does not fit): below the top row (the status cluster at the left, the screen menu ⋯ at the right), panes sit 40 px from the sides and 20 px apart, preferred widths 840, 342, 618, 640 and 1180 shrinking toward the minimums, stacked panes share the height equally, never under 240.
- **Dragging.** A grip shows at the top centre of the pane under the pointer; it is the browser's own drag and drop, so it crosses from one window to the other. While it is carried the pane leaves its place and the others make room; over a pane, the top quarter puts it above, the bottom quarter below, elsewhere the left or right half a new column on that side (over the top row, a gap or a margin there is no zone). A skeleton of the pane at its real size shows where it would land, and a chip with its name follows the pointer; while the pointer is over the skeleton the target holds. The drop is exactly that layout, at once in both windows. A drop that would push any pane below its minimum shows no skeleton and changes nothing. A drop (or a menu move) that gives today's arrangement saves no layout, so both screens go back to today's own geometry. The skeleton and the chip are drawn, not layers: they take no pointer and are gone with the drop.
- **The screen menu** (⋯, a floating layer): "Move <pane> to the <other> screen" for each pane on this screen (a column of its own at the end, or the bottom of a column when the width is gone), and "Reset the layout". **An empty screen** says "Nothing on this screen", offers the other screen's panes as buttons, and takes a drop anywhere.
- **Across windows** what one pane asks of another (open a ticket, a brief, a handoff, a draft; find a ticket; a conversation chosen; an agent clicked on the map) is an intent the daemon passes to the other window and keeps nowhere, so it works with the two panes on different screens. What the Sprint pane shows (the map, an open ticket, a brief, a handoff, the create form's draft) follows it to the other window and through a rearrangement of its own, as an intent too; nothing else of a pane does (a ticket's scroll, a half-typed form, the terminals' splits keep no more than a reload keeps).
- **Folding, removing and adding (free layout).** Each pane keeps today's hide button where it is today (Sprint's header, Elfoqsy's and the conversation's top right, the map's corner, the terminals' header), at one height on a screen: 24 px under the pane's top. Folding shows today's rail for the pane, unchanged (Sprint's orbit, story-point bar and blocked dot; Elfoqsy's avatar and agent badges in her panel; the conversation's icon and waiting dot; the map's Elfoqsy face and waiting dot; the terminals' count): 56 px wide and the screen's height, never grown. A pane folded inside a stack is a bar, 48 px high and as wide as its column, with the same elements in a row; the open panes of the stack share the height that is left. The show button of a rail or bar opens it. Which panes a window has folded is that window's own state, as it always was (`panes/folds.ts`): not saved, the same in today's columns and in a layout, and a pane that goes to the other window arrives open. What is saved always fits with everything open, so folding is a way to see less, never a way to have more, and a folded pane can always be opened.
- **The icon draws the layout.** The hide and show icon (26 × 18) is a miniature of the screen it is on: one bar per column, a thin bar for a folded column, a column split into a cell for each pane stacked in it; the pane's own cell is filled when it is open and dashed when it is folded. For two or three columns of one pane it is today's icon, number for number.
- **x** sits right of the hide button, the same size and style, and shows while the pointer is over the pane (it is always there for the keyboard): it takes the pane off the screens, and its place closes up. A layout may leave a pane on neither screen; it comes back through a + beside a pane, an empty screen's buttons, or the screen menu ("Add Conversation to this screen"). A folded pane has no x: open it first.
- **One + at the edge nearest the pointer.** Within 48 px of an open pane's edge (from 30 px outside it) a single + (26 px) shows in the gap at that edge's middle; at the top edge it is right of the grip, so the two never meet. It opens a menu, a floating layer (one at a time, closes on a click outside and on Escape), of the panes not on that screen, each marked "not on a screen", "moves from the other screen" or "no room" (disabled where the result would push a pane below its minimum). Choosing one places it on that side, as a drop there would. The grip, 52 × 24, straddles the pane's top edge, centred, on hover, on a rail or a bar too (a folded pane that is dragged is open where it lands). Today's screens, with no layout saved, offer the grip, the x and the + too, drawn over the panes by the screen, so today's panes are untouched and look as they always did at rest: the x, the same size as the hide button, 8 px from it, left of it (right of it for Elfoqsy, whose header has her other controls on the left), shown while the pointer is over the pane; the + at the edge nearest the pointer, from the boxes the page has, with the menu of the other screen's panes. The first use saves a layout (today's arrangement, changed), as the first drag does, and the screens go to the layout engine's geometry; Reset puts today's back, with the x and the + still offered.
- **Tested** by the size lab (`/panes?pane=sprint&w=500&h=620`, a corner to drag): a browser check run on every pane in a grid of sizes around each threshold, and after every step of the real-use script, fails on a font size that is not a step of the type scale, anything outside its slot, content wider or taller than a box that does not scroll, and two controls or texts that cross.

## Abilities


- **The view** fills the screen it was opened on (1920 × 1080) and is that window's own state, like its folds (`ui/abilitiesView.ts`): not saved, not relayed. The panes stay behind it as they are, a half-typed message included, and it hides the grip and the +. It opens from the Abilities button in Elfoqsy's pane (the third control of her top row) and from the small card of her notice, on that card. It closes on Escape (once no floating layer is open above it) and with its × in the top right; the prototype draws no way back, and a whole-screen view needs one.
- **Left column:** "Abilities" at 30/800 and the steps saved this month (24/800, "steps saved this month" muted 14px); the tools as filters, each a 40 px pill with its official logo (22 px) and count, "All" first, the chosen one white; then the cards, 232 × 325, 20 px apart, five to a row.
- **A card:** a raised surface (`--gray-900`, radius `--radius-lg`) with its tool's official logo faded behind it (90% of its width, at 42% of its height, turned −8°, 16% opacity, in the brand colour; a neutral tile's logo in the primary text colour), a shade toward the bottom, the level in a ring at the top left (44 px, 3 px stroke, filled toward the next level: 1, 3, 8, 15 and 30 verified uses that worked), the title at 18/800 at the bottom, and who found it at the bottom right: Elfoqsy's face, or the agent's number on her colour (26 px). A red dot (12 px) at the top right when it needs a re-check. The chosen card has a 2 px white edge.
- **Right column (520 px):** the chosen card larger (250 × 350, its ring 56 px, its title 22/800) beside its numbers at 30/800 with a muted 13px label: uses, worked (a percent, shown only once it has a use: nothing, not even a dash, before), steps saved ("~N"), and questions avoided when there are any. Under them its steps, one short line each: a 24 px numbered circle, the line at 15px, its sample as inline code (ellipsised). Under a hairline, where it came from in one line at 13px, muted and cut at the column's width (the whole of it in its tip): who found it and when, how often and where, what the friction cost, from what its ability.json keeps ("Found by Elfoqsy, 20 Oct · 24 times in 30 sessions and 10 runs · 5 calls, 1 failed"; an agent's "Found by Agent 5 on SHOP-3567, 12 Oct · 2 earlier runs · 9 calls, 2.4 failed, 0.6 questions"; hers, in the conversation she wrote it from, with what she wrote of it). Her notice of a new ability is a message in the conversation, not part of this view. Nothing else: details live behind the card.
- **Her notice in the conversation:** the message she writes when she learns an ability (her own words, in his language, never assembled by the workspace) carries an `ability` block for each; it shows as the small card (72 × 100, its ring 28 px and its finder 18 px with its number at 12px, no title) beside her line, or, for several, a row of them over it. On a panel the small card is one surface up (`--bg-overlay`). Each opens the view on its card. A new ability is level 1 (written and verified), its ring empty; its links and the AWS tool by their icons as everywhere.
- **Tested** by `apps/web/test/abilities.test.tsx` (the cards, the view and the notice as markup) and the browser tests' "A real use" (two windows at a person's pace; the floating layers and every pane's box checked after each step; the prototype's geometry measured).

## Left screen: sprint orbit map and Elfoqsy

Reference: `prototypes/left-screen.html`.

### Orbit map (left two thirds)

- Drawn in a 760×600 coordinate space scaled by 1.35, offset left so the detail panel never covers it.
- **Center:** a white 18px dot labelled "you".
- **Three rings = Jira priority**, labelled on their right side: Critical (r 120), Major (r 210), Minor (r 290). Blocker sits with Critical.
- **Each open ticket is a circle on its priority ring.** Diameter = 24 + 6 × story points (unestimated = 24). Fill = status colour. Glyph = type. Spread tickets around the ring so labels never overlap; keep the layout stable between refreshes (hash the key to an angle, then resolve collisions).
- **Linked tickets on different boards** (for example SHOP-3546 and CORE-18891) are joined by a dashed `#8dc2fe` line.
- **Pull requests are moons:** a thin orbit (`1px #444E4D`, about 34px radius) around the ticket the PR belongs to, with one 20px green (`#16a356`) circle per open PR carrying the pull-request glyph. Every PR belongs to a ticket; there is no free-floating PR. Hover shows repo and number.
- **Done tickets** are small green dots in the lower left corner; **cancelled** tickets are hollow rings in the upper left corner. No text; hover for details.

### Interaction

- Click a ticket: it gets a `0 0 0 4px #050606, 0 0 0 6px #ECEFEE` selection ring, and the right column switches from Elfoqsy to the ticket's details. Click again or press × to go back.
- Details panel, top to bottom: type glyph, key (links to Jira), ×; full summary; chips for status (status colour), priority, story points; facts grid (Agent, Epic, Sprint, Carried over, Reporter, Created, Updated, Labels, Linked); description, scrolling; flat sections separated by hairlines, no nested cards.
- "Carried over" turns `#e8d48a` from 6 sprints up.

### The full ticket

- **Top row, as before:** the dial on the left, and the arrows with the count under it ("not in sprint" for a ticket opened from elsewhere). On the right: "Parent" with the epic's key (an in-app link) and name, ellipsised; the type tile and key (a link to Jira) at 22px; chips for the workflow status (its real name, coloured by Jira's category: grey To Do, blue In Progress, green Done, grey for a dropped done status), the priority, the points, "Blocked by N" with a red dot (only for open "is blocked by" links) and Flagged; the title at 24px; "Created 8 Oct 2026 by Quinn Bauer · Updated 12 Oct 2026" in muted 14px.
- **Under it, scrolling inside the column**, after a hairline:
  - **Fields:** four columns (label 96px, value; label, value) at 17px, muted labels. Only fields with a value; the sprint's goal muted under its name; labels as round chips; "Carried over" in yellow when carried. "N empty fields", folded, lists the layout's empty fields on the ticket's edit screen, each "None".
  - **Rich text:** section titles at 17px/800 in secondary text; the text at 18px, line height 1.6; Jira headings a step larger; inline code as everywhere. An empty one shows "Add text" in a muted box (Design, a Figma field here, shows "No design linked").
  - **Other fields** (a label and value grid), **Sub-tasks**, **Linked work items** (grouped by Jira's wording; one grid for all rows: key, title on one line, status chip, priority icon, assignee initials), **Confluence** (the page chip and "mentions this ticket"), **Development**, **Attachments** (images inline up to 360px tall, other files as a row with name, size and Open), **Comments** (initials, name, time, the body), **History** (one line, "History · N changes", until opened; then a person's changes on one day as one entry, newest first: their name, how long ago, and each change, "changed Priority from Major to Blocker"; Rank and the automation's changes are left out; a long text's change reads "changed Acceptance criteria" with Show diff, which opens a line diff in the terminal's diff style, a little context around each change and the rest folded to "…").
- **Click to edit, select to read:** a rich text, a field value or the title enters edit mode only on a plain click, about 450 ms after it (the time a double click takes): not at the end of a drag, not on a double or triple click, not when the click leaves text selected. A link or ticket key inside opens; it never edits. Enter or Space on the focused value edits at once. The text of every value can be selected and copied.
- **Editing:** rich text opens in place in a bordered editor: a toolbar (Normal text ▾, B, I, </>, link, •≡, 1≡, table, { }, and @ in comments), the text at 17px, and a footer with the conflict note on the left ("TH Quinn Bauer changed Solution while you were editing") and See theirs, Cancel and a white Save on the right. Save after a conflict puts "Replace Quinn Bauer's version?" (bold, 14px) with Keep editing and a white Replace in their place; field values and the title show the same under their control. Field values edit in place with their own control and Save. The status chip has a ▾ and opens a menu: each transition's name on the left, its target status chip on the right. A field changed by someone else gets a lighter row and "Quinn Bauer, 10:20" for a few seconds; one Elfoqsy filled gets "Elfoqsy, 10:20" and Undo.
- **The dial, B+:** at rest only the open ticket is named (its digits, mono 13px). Hovered, or reached by keyboard, it grows 1.7× from its top left over the header, on the canvas with a hairline, and every dot shows its digits; a dot's own hover shows its title and workflow status in a small dark tip. No animation for reduced motion. The arrows show the digits of the ticket each goes to ("2091 ‹ 1 of 7 › 3049"). Under the expand button, a magnifier; "Find a ticket Ctrl K" shows beside it on hover only, as the grown dial's tooltip. The grown dial and its tips follow "Floating layers" above, and its labels are placed clear of every dot and label (a label that had to go farther away is tied to its dot by a hairline).
- **Find a ticket:** centred on the left screen near its top, 720px, over a backdrop that dims the screen: the query at 17px with "Esc", then rows of 46px: type tile, key in mono, title ellipsised, workflow chip, and a hairline tag "Sprint 52" or "not in sprint"; the chosen row on the selected surface. "New ticket" closes the list.
- **New ticket:** in the sprint column, a bordered card: "New ticket" and "Nothing reaches Jira until you press Create"; Project, Type (with its tile), Priority (with its icon) in three columns; Summary; Description in the editor; Sprint, Story points, Labels (chips); Links in Jira's wording; Assignee; Jira's other required fields; Cancel and a white Create.
- **A drafted ticket:** a raised block: the type tile and "New Bug · SHOP", "waiting for you" with the yellow dot on the right; the title at 18px/800; its main fields as hairline tags; Create in Jira (white), Edit first, Discard. Once closed: "Created as SHOP-9001" or "Discarded".
- **Jira news:** a digest block, "In Jira since 09:12" and "your tickets and mentions", rows of 44px with hairlines: initials, the name in bold, what, the key (mono, underlined, opens in Elfoqsy), the time on the right.
- **Inline code:** everywhere, also in tables, the editor and the terminals: mono, white on the selected surface, 2px 7px, radius 5px. Only code blocks unset it.
- **Icons, never letters.** One registry (`apps/web/src/ui/toolIcons.ts`, files and licences in `assets/tool-icons/SOURCES.md`): a tool by its name (the tile of a call, of her activity line, of an ability, of a connector), a link by its domain (`ui/linkIcons.ts`: Jira, Confluence, GitHub, Coralogix, AWS, Kubernetes and the rest, 16 px before the link's words, in ADF text, markdown, her messages and source chips), an attachment by its type (20 px) and a pull request's and its CI's state by GitHub's own icons (Octicons, in the state's colour). Atlassian's product icons are drawn whole, in their own colours, with no tile behind them. A tool with no official file (Black Duck, Zephyr) has a generic icon for its kind, and so has any tool or connector nobody has named; nothing is ever drawn from text. `test/icons.test.ts` fails if a proxy, a tool of a run, an activity, an ability tool or a connector has no icon, or if a file is unused or has no source and licence written down; the browser test "Icons audit" fails if a letter stands in for an icon on any screen it visits. People's avatars and the agents' numbers still show initials or digits.
- **Jira's rich text:** panels are their icon beside the text, never a fill or a stripe; a quotation is set in, never barred; colour marks are dropped; tables have hairline cells; links to tickets open in Elfoqsy, Confluence links are a chip with the page's icon and title, other links open in a new tab, and anything else that cannot be shown is named ("[extension shown in Jira]").

### Elfoqsy column (right third, 480px)

- Her portrait fills the top as a square. Expression follows the most urgent state: `broken.png` when something is broken, `needs-you.png` when something waits for the user, `working.png` while agents work, `quiet.png` otherwise.

## Right screen: live agent map

Reference: `prototypes/right-screen.html` (the canvas code there is the starting point).

- **Elfoqsy is the core:** her portrait in a 112px circle with a Skobeloff ring at (760, 560), a soft breathing Skobeloff glow behind it.
- **Each agent** sits 290px out on its own thread from the core, as a Skobeloff circle numbered in order of creation, wrapped by **one arc segment per step of the agent's own plan** (`run.plan_declared`; a single grey ring until it declares one; the simulation's agents declare Plan, Code, Test, PR, CI, Review, Merge): done green, current step pulsing blue while working, yellow when waiting for the user, red at the moment of a failure, the rest `#1a2121`.
- **Its ticket** sits 500px out on the same direction, drawn exactly like on the left screen, joined to the agent by a dashed line.
- **Sub-agents** spawn out of the agent into a fan toward the ticket, spin while working, flash green or red when they finish, then shrink back into the agent. A green or red dot then travels back along the thread to Elfoqsy: that is the report.
- **Instructions** are small Skobeloff dots flowing from Elfoqsy out to working agents.

## Handoff, verdict and the weekly review

- **Handoff card** (block `handoff`): the brief card's shape: a document glyph, "Handoff: <KEY>", one line with the mode and what it carries ("hotfix, 1 message of his, 1 constraint, 3 facts, 1 left out"), Open, and a row per agent started from it.

## Terminals, questions and decisions

**Design rule, everywhere: no coloured stripe beside a card, block or row; status shows only as an icon or a dot.** A highlighted code line gets a slightly lighter row and brighter text, nothing else. `apps/web/test/v13.test.tsx` and the browser check (`pnpm --filter @elfoqsy/web e2e`) fail if a stripe comes back; a 1px neutral divider between columns is not one.

- **Pane**: a rounded panel, the focused one outlined in Skobeloff. Header 56px: badge, ticket (or a task's name), model and effort in muted text, the steps as dots (done green, failed red, abandoned grey, the current one blue with a ring, yellow while it waits) and the current step's name, the sub-agent count, and ⋯ for split, timeline, pause and close.
- **Body**: prose first, the agent's text as Markdown at 16px (15px in a narrow pane), line height 1.7 (`--type-prose-leading`), at most 68ch (`--type-prose-measure`). Each tool call is one light line: an 18px tile, the name in bold, the call in muted mono, a status icon and the duration; one line of its result under it, indented. A web read shows its domain. An Edit keeps its numbered diff. Three or more calls in a row that went well are one chip: up to four 16px tiles, "6 reads and searches" or "4 commands", a check and ▸; it opens on click. A failed or refused call is never in a chip.
- **The verdict** is one line, a check in a circle, the label and a muted detail ("reproduced"), its findings as plain points under it.
- **Elfoqsy's answer**: her face, "Elfoqsy answered for you · from <sources>", the answer, Change (the other options).
- **A question for him** ends the pane: "needs you" with a yellow dot, the question in bold, the options as buttons ("label: consequence").
- **Footer**: a full-size input "Message agent N" and the tokens against the budget; a muted line above it when the run is paused or flagged.
- **The question card in chat**: agent badge, "Agent 2 · SHOP-2091", "needs you"; the question at 18px bold; the why points; the options in a grid, each its label in bold and its consequence in muted text, Elfoqsy's suggestion marked "· Elfoqsy suggests" with a lighter border; "N sources" collapsed (her reason inside); "Let Elfoqsy decide this kind next time".
- **The digest**: "I decided N things while you were away" ("I decided N things since 09:12" when he opened it himself with the checklist button beside the settings in Elfoqsy's column; "opened by the tester" when the tester did), "all reversible" when they are; a row per decision with a green check, what she decided, its ticket and what it rests on in muted text, and View, Undo or Change.
- **The delegation sheet**: over Elfoqsy's panel; each category with Decide, Decide and tell me, Ask me; precedents with Remove; agents at once; how many of her decisions he changed.
- **A new agent's pane** opens by itself beside the focused one if a split fits, else in place of another pane (never the focused one): a finished agent's or an empty one first, one waiting for him last, the least recently focused among equals. The pane taken over starts afresh on the log. It never takes focus. Clicking an agent on the map (or Tab and Enter on it) focuses its pane, opening it if needed, and the terminals column too when it is hidden. Four agents start in a 2x2 grid.
